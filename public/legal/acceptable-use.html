PLAIN-LANGUAGE RULE. When a situation is unclear, protect the other people at the table and ask Support before acting.

1. Treat people as people

Do not harass, threaten, stalk, target, or discriminate against another player. Do not use slurs, hateful imagery, sexual exploitation, or personal information as a weapon. A competitive table can be sharp without becoming hostile.

2. Play the game, not the client

Do not alter network messages, browser state, requests, assets, or scripts to change ownership, cash, movement, dice, contracts, auctions, market outcomes, casino outcomes, rewards, or rankings. Do not use automation except for bot features explicitly provided by Poorup. Do not manipulate disconnects or reconnects to bypass a decision.

3. Protect the service

Do not probe accounts, steal sessions, brute-force credentials, flood sockets, bypass rate limits, scrape private surfaces, distribute malware, or test a vulnerability against another player. Stop testing when you confirm a weakness and report it privately through Support.

4. Keep names, avatars, and chat safe

Do not impersonate another person or organization. Do not submit illegal material, confidential material you do not have permission to share, personal data about another person, or links intended to mislead or infect. Poorup may remove content that creates a safety, legal, or operational risk.

5. Enforcement

The operator may warn, mute, remove content, end a room, restrict an account, or preserve evidence when needed to protect the service or comply with law. The final policy will state the available notice, duration, review, and account-data handling for each action. A moderation action must not be used to punish a good-faith security report.

6. Reports

Use the Support notice for harassment, cheating, privacy, or security reports. Include the smallest useful amount of information: approximate time, room or match reference, what happened, and any safe reproduction steps. Never publish credentials, session tokens, private messages, or a working exploit in a public room.

7. Appeals and good-faith mistakes

The effective policy will name an appeal channel and response target. If you made a mistake without intending harm, stop, preserve the relevant context, and tell Support. A good-faith report or appeal should not itself be treated as abuse.
